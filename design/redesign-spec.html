PathwayUSA — screen redesign

Seven screens, restructured around one rule: every screen answers "what do I do next?" before it shows anything else. Voice is treated as a first-class navigation layer, not an accessibility afterthought.

What changed

  • Countdown is the hero. Computed from the green card date they already give you (5 years, or 3 if married to a citizen, minus the 90-day early filing window). This is the single most emotionally sticky thing you can put on screen — people open the app just to look at it.
  • Two CTAs became one. "Continue Journey" and "Continue Step" pointed to the same place.
  • Progress is now a real measure. "You'd pass 14 of 20" moves when they study. "18% of journey" moves when they tick a checkbox.
  • Removed: duplicate progress card, six-tile grid, badge counter, stats bar, testimonial, horizontal journey cards, dark CTA banner.
Before you ship: the "2.1M+ / 15K+ / 500+" stats and the "Ana L." testimonial with the cartoon avatar have to go unless they're real. Fabricated review content is an App Store rejection risk, and your audience is the most scam-targeted population in the country — synthetic-looking proof triggers exactly the alarm you don't want.

Empty state

  • Before a green card date exists, the navy header becomes a single prompt: "Add your green card date to see when you can apply." One field, one button. That's the highest-value thing a new user can do.

What changed

  • One continuous list, not tabs. Steps / Timeline / Tasks split one mental model into three. Completed steps collapse to a single dimmed line; the active step expands. That's the entire interaction.
  • Done steps stay visible. Seeing two finished steps above you is the motivation that a "18%" number tries and fails to deliver.
  • "What do I need for this?" is the entry point for your RAG assistant — scoped to the current step, so answers stay grounded instead of open-ended.

Language check: "Est. time: Study 2–3 months before interview" reads as a promise about their case. "Most people study 2–3 months" is the same information without implying you know their timeline.

Why this matters most

  • Switching cost. A user who has uploaded five documents does not re-upload them somewhere else. Nothing else in the app creates that.
  • Expiry tracking is the notification nobody mutes. "Renewal window opens in 1 month" is worth a push. "Come practice!" is not.
  • Status pills, not just presence. Saved / Needed / Expiring, each with an icon as well as color — never color alone.
  • Camera-first capture. Your users are photographing paper, not attaching PDFs from a desktop.
Security is non-negotiable here. No permanent public URLs, signed short-lived links only, file type and size validation, sanitized filenames, and nothing about document contents in logs or analytics. And only claim encryption on this screen if it's actually true end-to-end — a false trust badge is worse than none.

Copy that has to change

Design principles

  • Same position on every screen. The play control sits in one consistent place. A user who can't read English reliably learns "the button in that spot talks to me" — that's the whole onboarding.
  • It reads the step, not the interface. Reading button labels aloud is noise. Reading the plain-language explanation of what this step means is the value.
  • Voice input, not just output. Typing a question in a second language is a real barrier. Speaking one isn't. This is where your RAG assistant belongs — scoped to the current step so answers stay grounded in your content.
  • Written text stays visible while playing. Users following along build reading skill; users who can't still get the audio. Never make voice the only path to information.

Hard problems to plan for

  • Haitian Creole TTS is genuinely poor across most major providers. Test it with native speakers before you ship it as a feature. Shipping bad Creole audio to Creole speakers is worse than shipping none — it signals you didn't check.
  • Pre-generate, don't synthesize live. Roadmap steps are fixed content. Generate the audio once, store the files, serve them. Live TTS on every open costs money per user, breaks offline, and adds latency on poor connections — which your audience disproportionately has.
  • Offline matters. Cached audio for the current step means the app works on the bus with no signal.
  • Never auto-play. A phone that starts talking about immigration status in a public place can put someone in a genuinely unsafe position. Always user-initiated.
On the RAG assistant: it must refuse to answer case-specific eligibility questions. "Do I qualify?" and "will I be approved?" have to route to a licensed attorney every time, not get a confident-sounding generated answer. Constrain it to explaining your own content and the screen the user is on.

Build order

Ranked by risk removed and value delivered, not by effort.

  1. Remove the fabricated content. Fake stats, fake testimonial, "increase your approval chances," "USCIS Compliance." Copy-only, no engineering, removes real legal and store-review risk today.
  2. Fix the Home hierarchy. Eligibility countdown as hero, one CTA, three list rows. Biggest perceived-quality jump for the least work.
  3. Consolidate color tokens. Teal = progress, amber = time-sensitive, red = errors and destructive only. Touches every screen but is mechanical.
  4. Tag civics questions by topic. Schema migration. Unlocks the readiness diagnostic, which is your real daily habit.
  5. Build documents properly. Expiry tracking plus secure storage. Your strongest retention mechanic and your biggest security surface — treat them as one project.
  6. Ship voice on the roadmap steps. Pre-generated audio, English first, one consistent control position. Validate Creole with native speakers before promising it.
  7. Add the scoped RAG assistant last. It needs the content structure from the steps above to be grounded, and it needs hard refusal rules for eligibility questions before it touches a user.
